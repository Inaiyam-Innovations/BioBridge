1. About BioBridge

BioBridge – Bio Innovation Hackathon is an innovation-focused hackathon designed to encourage students to identify, understand, and develop practical solutions to real-world challenges in biotechnology and allied sectors.

The event brings together biology, biotechnology, healthcare, agriculture, environmental science, food technology, bioinformatics, artificial intelligence, engineering, design, entrepreneurship, and other interdisciplinary domains to encourage the development of technology-enabled biological solutions.

BioBridge is structured around multiple challenge areas covering healthcare, diagnostics, genomics, agriculture, food, environmental biotechnology, industrial biotechnology, and emerging biological applications.

The objective of BioBridge is not limited to software development or conventional laboratory research. Participants are encouraged to think across disciplines and develop solutions that demonstrate:

  • Scientific relevance
  • Practical applicability
  • Innovation
  • Technical feasibility
  • Responsible biological innovation
  • Social or environmental impact
  • Scalability
  • Potential for real-world implementation
  • Entrepreneurship and deployment potential

The hackathon provides participants with an opportunity to transform an identified biological or biotechnology-related challenge into a structured solution concept, prototype, technology demonstration, research pathway, or deployable innovation.

Participation in BioBridge is subject to the eligibility criteria, submission requirements, timelines, safety requirements, evaluation procedures, and other conditions specified in these Terms & Conditions.

3. Nature of the Event

BioBridge is an innovation and problem-solving hackathon. Depending on the selected challenge and the participant's proposed approach, a solution may involve one or more of the following:

SoftwareArtificial intelligence / Machine learningBioinformaticsComputational biologyData scienceDigital healthDiagnosticsHardwareSensorsIoTEmbedded systemsBiotechnology workflowsLaboratory conceptsSimulationDigital twinsMobile / Web appsResearch methodologiesAgricultural biotechnologyFood biotechnologyEnvironmental biotechnologyIndustrial biotechnologySustainable biological processesProduct conceptsBusiness modelsHybrid technology solutions

Participants are not required to use a particular programming language, framework, platform, laboratory technique, or technology stack unless specifically required by the selected problem statement.

4. Eligibility

BioBridge is intended primarily for student innovators and interdisciplinary teams interested in biotechnology and allied fields. Participants should provide accurate academic and institutional information during registration.

Students from different academic disciplines may participate, provided that their proposed solution is relevant to the selected BioBridge challenge. Relevant disciplines include:

Biotechnology · Bioinformatics · Microbiology · Biochemistry · Biomedical Engineering · Medicine and allied health sciences · Agriculture · Agricultural Engineering · Food Technology · Chemical Engineering · Environmental Science · Life Sciences · Computer Science · AI & ML · Information Technology · Electronics & Communication · Electrical Engineering · Mechanical Engineering · Design · Management · Entrepreneurship · Data Science.

Interdisciplinary participation is encouraged.

The organisers reserve the right to verify participant eligibility at any stage of the event. Submission of false, misleading, incomplete, or unverifiable information may result in disqualification.

5. Team Participation

BioBridge shall be conducted through team-based participation. The official application specification provides for a team size of 2 to 4 members. Each team must register its members through the designated registration process.

5.1 Team Size

Minimum: 2 members · Maximum: 4 members. A team may not exceed the maximum permitted number of members.

5.2 Single Institution Requirement

All members of a team must belong to the same college or institution. A participant may not simultaneously represent multiple teams for the same problem statement or hackathon submission where such participation creates a conflict with the official event rules.

5.3 Team Lead

Each team must nominate one member as the Team Lead, who acts as the primary point of contact for registration, submission communication, shortlisting announcements, queries, presentation coordination, and clarifications.

5.4 Team Member Information

Each member may be required to provide full name, email address, mobile number, college/institution, course/programme, and year of study.

5.5 Changes to Team Composition

Once registration closes, changes to team composition are restricted. Any request to add, remove, or replace a member is considered only at the sole discretion of the organisers.

6. Registration

6.1 No Registration Fee

There is No registration fee, No application fee, No participation fee, and No payment required to submit an entry. Participants should not make any payment to any individual claiming to represent BioBridge unless officially communicated.

6.2 Registration Information

Participants must complete the official registration form within the specified window. It collects team lead details, institution details, team members, category selection, problem statement selection, wildcard details where applicable, and the one-pager document.

6.3 Accurate Information

Participants are responsible for ensuring all submitted details are accurate, complete, current, verifiable, and belong to the actual participating individuals.

7. Problem Statement Structure

The official problem statement document contains 36 problem statements across six core categories and an Open Bio Track:

7.1 Track BB-PB

Precision Biotherapeutics & Drug Discovery

Drug discovery, repurposing, protein structures, molecular interactions, AMR, antimicrobial peptides, biologics.

7.2 Track BB-DM

Diagnostics & Medical Devices

Low-cost diagnostics, screening, medical devices, point-of-care technologies, digital health, clinical decision support.

7.3 Track BB-BG

Bioinformatics, Genomics & Bio-AI

Genomics, variant analysis, bioinformatics, eDNA, pharmacogenomics, biomedical artificial intelligence, AI reliability.

7.4 Track BB-CA

Climate-Resilient Agriculture & Agri-Biotech

Agricultural biotechnology, crop health, biological inputs, soil health, traceability, tissue culture, climate resilience.

7.5 Track BB-FN

Functional Foods, Nutrition & Smart Proteins

Alternative proteins, food biotechnology, fermentation, food safety, nutrition, edible coatings, food adulteration.

7.6 Track BB-EW

Environmental, Industrial & Waste-to-Value Biotech

Bioremediation, waste valorisation, plastic degradation, industrial biotechnology, groundwater monitoring, agro-residue utilisation.

7.7 Track BB-OB

Open Bio Track

Space life support, biosafety compliance, regulatory pathways, herbal authentication, virtual labs, and participant-proposed Wildcard.

8–10. Selection, Codes & Wildcard

8. Problem Statement Selection

Each team selects one problem statement. Once shortlisted, it is treated as locked unless permission is explicitly granted. Solutions must directly address the selected challenge.

9. Problem Statement Codes

Follows BB-[TRACK]-[NUMBER] (e.g. BB-PB-01, BB-DM-01, BB-BG-01, BB-CA-01, BB-FN-01, BB-EW-01, BB-OB-01). Must be quoted accurately on all submissions.

10. Open Bio Track – Wildcard

Teams may propose their own challenge, which must be genuinely biological, defined, safe, and lawful. Wildcard ideas are reviewed by organisers prior to approval.

11–12. Round 1 – Idea Submission & Deadlines

11. Round 1 – Idea Submission

Teams submit a structured one-pager in PDF format following the supplied template (max 5 MB).

Should communicate: Problem understanding, target user/beneficiary, proposed solution, biological/scientific basis, technology approach, innovation, impact, feasibility, implementation, scalability. Recommended filename: TeamName_StatementCode.pdf.

12. Submission Deadlines

Submissions must be completed before the deadline. Technical issues or device errors do not guarantee extensions.

13–15. Evaluation, Build Phase & AI Tools

13. Evaluation & Shortlisting

Evaluated by an expert panel of faculty, researchers, industry specialists, and entrepreneurs. Panel decisions are final.

14. Hackathon / Build Phase

Shortlisted teams advance to the 4-week build sprint with 4 mandatory online weekend sessions, developing prototypes, decks, and validation pathways.

15. Use of AI and Digital Tools

AI tools are permitted, but participants are strictly accountable for scientific claims, data accuracy, and citations. Fabricated citations lead to immediate disqualification.

16–18. Biosafety, Health Solutions & Data

16. Biological Safety and Responsible Innovation

Biosafety and responsible innovation are fundamental. No unauthorised laboratory experiments, handling of hazardous biological agents, or unapproved human/animal testing. BioBridge does not provide external laboratory access or regulatory permits.

17. Medical and Health-Related Solutions

Must distinguish between prototypes/concepts and clinically validated medical products. Any medical decision-support must acknowledge the requirement for professional clinician involvement.

18. Data, Privacy and Confidential Information

Do not submit confidential patient records, unanonymised genetic data, or proprietary third-party datasets without authorization.

19–22. Originality, IP Rights & Third-Party Tech

19. Originality and Plagiarism

Work must be original. Plagiarism, data fabrication, or copying other teams results in immediate disqualification.

20. Intellectual Property Rights

Participants retain ownership of intellectual property that they independently create, subject to existing institutional policies or open-source licences.

21. Third-Party Technology

APIs, open-source libraries, and models must comply with their respective licences.

22. Project Ownership & Team Responsibility

The team is collectively responsible for internal arrangements regarding contribution and IP.

23–26. Conduct, Fair Participation & Platforms

23–24. Conduct & Fair Participation

Professional conduct, mutual respect, and fairness are strictly enforced. Cheating, bribery, impersonation, or platform abuse will trigger immediate removal.

25–26. Communication & Platforms

Official communication flows through email, Unstop, and the official website. Participants are responsible for having standard laptop and internet resources.

27–30. Presentations, Jury, Mentoring & Certificates

27–28. Presentation & Jury Evaluation

Shortlisted teams pitch before a grand jury evaluated on problem understanding, scientific relevance, innovation, feasibility, impact, scalability, and responsible innovation.

29–30. Mentoring & Certificates

Mentoring offers guidance without commercial guarantee. Participation certificates are awarded to eligible teams who satisfy all requirements.

35. Format Modification: Organisers reserve the right to modify schedules, rounds, or formats when administratively required.

36. Force Majeure: Delays caused by natural disasters, government restrictions, or outages will be rescheduled where reasonably possible.

37–39. Photography, Media & Confidentiality: Event proceedings may be recorded for legitimate institutional and promotional purposes. Presentations are public; teams with patentable inventions should take precautions prior to demo day.

40. Limitation of Liability: Organisers and partner institutions are not liable for participants' personal equipment or data loss.

41–47. Regulatory Compliance, Claims & No Refund Policy

41. No Commercial Guarantee: Participation does not guarantee funding, employment, or commercialisation.

42. Regulatory Compliance: Participants are responsible for understanding applicable regulations (CDSCO, FSSAI, GEAC, BIS, etc.).

43–44. References & Claims: Credible scientific references must be acknowledged; teams are held strictly accountable for claims in their pitches.

45. Participant Data: Data collected is used solely for legitimate event administration.

46. Official Decisions: Operational and evaluation decisions by the organisers and jury panel are authoritative.

47. No Payment or Refund Policy: As BioBridge is 100% free with no fee at any stage, no refund policy applies.
